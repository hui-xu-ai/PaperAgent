# Reinforced Magnetic-Responsive Electro-Ionic Artificial Muscles by 3D Laser-Induced Graphene Nano-Heterostructures

Zhenjin Xu, Keqi Deng, Yang Zhang, Bin Zhu, Jianhui Yang, Mingcheng Xue, Hang Jin, Gonghan He, Gaofeng Zheng,Jianyi Zheng,\* and Dezhi Wu\*


## Abstract

Eficient ion transport and enriched responsive modals via modulating electrochemical properties ofconductivity and capacitance are essential for soft electro-ionic actuators. However, cost-efective and straightforward approaches to achieve expedited fabrication ofactive electrode materials capable ofmultimodal-responsiveness remain limited. Herein, this work reports the one-step ultrafast laser direct patterning method, to readily synthesize electro- and magneto-active electrode material, derived from the unique cobalt-phosphorus co-doped core–shell heterostructures within 3D graphene frameworks, for fulfilling the dual-mode responsive electro-ionic actuators. The designed nanofiber-structured heterointerfaces across electrodes and electrolytes further promote highly eficient electron/ion transfer. The developed soft actuator exhibits superior actuation performance ofpeak-to-peak displacement to 13.08 mm under an ultra-low ±0.5 V, with doubled direct current deflection under 200 mT at 1 V, an ultrafast response of 1.38 s and long-term stability (>90% retention for ≈106 000 cycles), even detectable bending to ≈280 μm under exceptional ±10 mV. The promising demonstration ofpromoting diferentiation and proliferation ofstem cells under mechanical strain and electrical stimuli, sheds more light as well on the possibility offacilitating biomedical soft robotics with ultrahigh actuation performance.




## 1. Introduction

Artificial muscles, designed to emulate the characteristics of natural muscles, manifest reversible transformations in posture or motion upon receiving tethered (e.g., electrical, fluidic) or untethered (e.g., magnetic, chemical, or temperature) external stimuli,<sup>[1–5]</sup> for possibly surpassing the inherent capabilities of their natural counterparts in real-world applications. Electro-ionic soft actuators, characterized by the lightweight, superior flexibility, high stroke, and ultralow working voltage, have emerged as promising candidates for artificial muscles, and demonstrate considerable potential in miniaturized robotics, functional structures, and biomedical science.<sup>[6–8]</sup> Notably, the active electrode material is responsible for the highperformance of soft actuators by regulating electron/ion transport; however, challenges and limitations persist in achieving a high specific capacitance alongside robust electrical conductivity.<sup>[9]</sup> Significant advancements have been made in the exploration and tailored design ofactive electrode nanomaterials, highlighted by carbon/MXenebased materials (e.g., carbon nanotubes, MXenes, and graphene) renowned for conductivity (≈20 000 S cm<sup>−1</sup>),<sup>[10–13]</sup> as well as hierarchical architecture-rich organic frameworks (e.g., covalent and metal–organic frameworks) for exceptional capacitance (≈247 F g<sup>−1</sup>).<sup>[14–17]</sup> However, seamlessly integrating the fundamental advantages of both for developing highperformance active electrode nanocomposites in a straightforward and eficient method via cost-efective procedures is challenging. Further, there is an inherent dificulty for soft actuators in achieving improvements in the blocking force (≈4.5 mN, 2.0 V) and actuation strain (≈0.68%, 1.0 V) mechanistically,<sup>[18,19]</sup> and the following demonstrations still fall considerably short ofpractical applicability.

Multi-modal actuation strategies have been adopted to facilitate the seamless expansion of electro-ionic soft actuators into complex and dynamic contexts.<sup>[20]</sup> Especially, in comparison to photoactive actuation inevitably sufered by its limited penetrability, the incorporation of magnetoactive properties facilitates the stimulation transmission, particularly in scenarios characterized by limited accessibility. Magnetically active materials have demonstrated considerable potential for achieving targeted delivery of therapeutics via miniaturized soft robotics requiring minimal interventional procedures.<sup>[21,22]</sup> However, the high remanence of hard ferromagnetic materials tends to induce spontaneous self-agglomeration, rendering the fabrication process challenging. Thus, harnessing a low voltage with appropriate magnetic stimuli to ofer a dual-mode responsive mechanism, will enhance the high-performance electrochemical actuation on functionality expansion and universality, opening the possibilities toward specialized biomedical applications.<sup>[23,24]</sup> To this end, developing dual-responsive active materials with high electrical conductivity, superior electro-ionic capacity, and optimal magnetic property in a facile manner will overcome the shortcomings ofconventional ferromagnetic materials in terms ofconductivity and capacitance.

Recently, direct laser writing (DLW) has garnered significant interest for eficient nano- and micro-scale patterning to fabricate 3D porous structures with highly conductive few-layered laserinduced graphene $( \mathrm { L I G } ) . ^ { [ 2 5 ] }$ Doping of magnetic metal atoms, particularly redox-active transition metal atoms, into metallic nanoparticles via DLW not only enhances the conductivity and introduces magnetic domains but also improves the electroionic capacitance. The participation of cobalt central has been extensively investigated for catalysis/electrocatalysis and energy storage applications,<sup>[26–28]</sup> and their superior reversible ion insertion/extraction enables overcoming the sole dependence on the electric double-layer capacitor (EDLC).<sup>[29]</sup> Phosphorus as dopants, owing to their larger radius than that of carbon atoms, increases the number of active sites by altering the surface and porosity of $\mathrm { L I G s } , ^ { [ 3 0 ] }$ and the lone-pair electrons in the $3 \mathrm { p }$ and vacant 3d orbitals of phosphorous are anticipated to attract local charges to potentially improve the electrical conductivity of transition metal compounds.<sup>[31]</sup> However, research on the energylevel alignment of functionally doped LIGs for fabricating dualmode magnetically responsive electro-ionic soft actuators is still lacking.

Herein, we report a one-step ultrafast DLW approach to facilely synthesize cobalt and phosphorus (Co/P) co-doped core–shell nanoparticles incorporated into 3D hierarchical nanoporous Pdoped LIG (P-LIG) frameworks (denoted as $\mathrm { C o ( O _ { x } / P _ { x } ) @ P { \cdot } L I G ) }$ featuring electro- and magneto-active electrode materials for high-performance dual-mode responsive soft ionic actuators. By regulating the doping heteroatoms to achieve desired magnetic properties, the synergistic coupling efect between the morphological structure and reduced redox-active center also significantly enhanced the electrochemical performance. In addition electrospun nanofiber-structured heterointerfaces between electrodes and electrolytes were employed to construct nano-fibrous proton exchange pathways to further promote electron/ion transfer. The achieved properties of the heterostructure improved the comprehensive actuation performance, showing peak-to-peak displacement to 13.08 mm under ±0.5 V, and even impressively bending up to ≈280 μm under ±10 mV, ultrafast response duration of 1.38 s and long-term stability (>90% retention) in ≈106 000 cycles. The nanoscale ${ \mathrm { C o } } ( \mathrm { O } _ { \mathrm { x } } / \mathrm { P } _ { \mathrm { x } }$ )-derived active material achieved a saturated magnetization of 9.80 $\mathsf { A } \mathsf { m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { ~ k g } ^ { - 1 }$ with a small remanence of1.01 $\mathsf { A } \mathsf { m } ^ { \bar { 2 } } \mathrm { k g } ^ { - 1 }$ , inducing suitable magnetoactive actuations for cellulose-based electro-ionic soft actuators. The proof-of-the-concept demonstration for biomedical-oriented soft robotics on inducing the differentiation and proliferation of mes enchymal stem cells (MSCs), providing simultaneous mechanical strain and electrical stimuli simultaneously with the developed dual-responsive soft actuators, highlights the potential for developing an automated culture platform with a compact size, simplified structure, biocompatible nature, and impressive performance.

## 2. Results and Discussion

## 2.1. Electron/Ion Transport Mechanism and Structure Design of Soft Actuator

By combining the pseudocapacitive properties oftransition metal phosphides/oxides with the superior electrical conductivity of graphene, this study aimed to demonstrate high-eficiency Co/P co-doped capacitive-conductive transport via a core–shell active electrode material, through the laser-induced P-doped defectrich 3D graphene frameworks. Analogous to catching fireflies with a net, electrons and ions were similarly apprehended within an electric field, facilitated by charge neutralization and ionic adsorption (Figure 1a). The modulation of the poly(amic acid) (PAA) precursor and laser scribing conditions contributed to the formation of coral-like macroscopic structures in graphene, which in turn, is advantageous for the transport and storage of electrons and ions. In the presence of an electric field, the narrow pores within the graphene-sheet and coral-like networks, by virtue of the confinement efect, ensnares sub-nanoscale ions (EMIM<sup>+</sup>, $\mathrm { B F _ { 4 } } ^ { - }$ of ionic liquid, IL).<sup>[32]</sup> The vacancies dispersed across the pore surface promoted the establishment of stable covalent linkages between the entities, in which localized alterations in electric charge within defect-rich graphene, also readily ensnaring electrons with electrostatic interaction.<sup>[33]</sup> Structurally, the integration ofthe core–shell coral-like graphitic skeleton with numerous structural unit cells coated in a ring-like manner with layers of P-doped graphene, facilitates rapid electron/ion transport within such porous configurations. The attachment of transition metal compounds enveloped by defect-laden graphene to 3D LIG frameworks significantly improved bidirectional conductivity and capacitance performance by harnessing capacitive potential of pseudocapacitive materials (Figure 1b).

Designing the heterointerface between electrolytes and electrodes in electrochemical actuators requires improving the structural compatibility and electron/ion transport,<sup>[34,35]</sup> which remains a critical challenge. In this work, a foundational fiber deposition substrate was initially established atop the prepared bacterial cellulose-based electrolyte membrane via dip-coating in a diluted Nafion solution, thereafter by direct electrospinning and high-temperature annealing to construct homogenous $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ -doped Nafion nanoscale frameworks upon this base, displaying modulation of the nanofiber-mat heterointerface with progressive structural changes. The process and characterization ofthe nanofiber interface modulation are described in detail in Figures 1c and S1 and S2 and Note S1 (Supporting Information). The proposed process not only provides nanoscopic deposition zones for the later application of flexible electrode coatings, thereby augmenting the structural interaction and robustness, but also enhances the electron/ion transport eficiency across the heterointerfaces.

Starting from the process involving laser induction of the PAA precursor to its imidization with $\scriptstyle \mathrm { C o } / \mathrm { P }$ co-doping, the physical appearance of the bare polyimide (PI) film not only transitioned from sap-green to blackish color but also rapidly developed into microporous frameworks. The resultant coral-like graphene networks with core–shell transition metal nanoparticles were transformed from the organic components of doped PI, resulting in superior electron/ion transfer capability by metallic form ofCo via laser-induced carbothermal reduction,<sup>[36]</sup> other than encasing it within and furnishing a supporting architecture ofLIG. The phosphides/oxides of Co have further improved the electrochemical and electron/ion storage capacities. Furthermore, the soft ferromagnetic characteristics of the $\mathrm { C o ( O _ { x } / P _ { x } ) @ P { \cdot } L I G }$ , attributed to metallic Co and ${ \mathrm { C o } } _ { 2 } { \mathrm { P } }$ , enabled the development of a dual-responsive actuator with exceptional electro- and magnetoactive actuation capabilities (Figure 1d). Owing to the microporous and coral-like core–shell structure, $\mathrm { C o ( O _ { x } / P _ { x } ) @ P { \cdot } L I G }$ facilitated the fabrication ofhomogeneous composite electrodes, and the incorporation of porous carbon prevented agglomeration of magnetic materials, facilitating more eficient dispersion within the electrodes.

The surface morphologies ofthe Co- and P-derived LIGs show the presence of coral-like porous frameworks of doped LIGs attached by transition metal nanoparticles-based core–shell structures. The structure of $\mathrm { C o ( O _ { x } / P _ { x } ) @ P { \cdot } L I G }$ comprises a central macropore, encircled by mesopores having a coral-like graphene architecture with branches sheathed under numerous nanoparticles (Figure 2a,b; Figure S3, Supporting Information). The ho mogeneous precursor film with the source elements, further processed via instantaneous high-energy laser induction, enabled the homogeneous dispersion of C, O, P, and $\mathrm { C o } ,$ , as evidenced by EDS analysis (Figure S4, Supporting Information). The crosssectional view of $\mathrm { C o ( O _ { x } / P _ { x } ) @ P { \cdot } L I G }$ resembles that of growing coral, where the macro-porous arrangement is dense and uni form, with thickness up to 37.3 μm (Figure 2c). By regulating the weight ratio of the Co and P sources in the precursor, the morphology of the doped LIG structures can be controlled during laser induction. Specifically, lower ratios resulted in smaller pores, thicker graphene layers, and shorter structures with less uniformity, and vice versa, albeit with structurally weaker architectures (Figures S5 and S6, Supporting Information). The core–shell graphite nanosheets were further evaluated using TEM analysis (Figure 2d), showing that the few-layered LIG nanosheets are uniformly interspersed with ${ \mathrm { C o } } ( \mathrm { O } _ { \mathrm { x } } / \mathrm { P } _ { \mathrm { x } } )$ nanoparticles having an average diameter of ≈14.76 nm. These nanoparticles are randomly separated and encapsulated by thermal de composed LIGs (Figure 2e), which further identified by the highresolution TEM (HRTEM) images (Figure 2f). The laser-induced nanocomposite of CoP core can be observed with clear stripes corresponding to (202), (210), and (201) planes of metallic $\mathrm { C o } ,$ CoP, and ${ \mathrm { C o } } _ { 2 } { \mathrm { P } }$ , respectively. In addition, the $\mathrm { C o O _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { X } } }$ core was also observed with lattice spacing of0.21 and 0.19 nm corresponding to Co (213) and CoO (102), respectively (Figure S7, Supporting Information). The selected area electron difraction pattern show difraction spots partially located on the concentric ring pattern corresponding to ${ \mathrm { C o } } ( \mathrm { O } _ { \mathrm { x } } / \mathrm { P } _ { \mathrm { x } } )$ polycrystallinity.<sup>[37]</sup> In addition, two dominant rings indexed as (100) and (002) are clearly visible, suggesting the high degree of crystallization and graphitization of polycrystalline P-doped LIG (Figure 2g).

The high-quality of the synthesized composite is confirmed by the distinct lattice patterns observed in pristine and P-doped graphene (Figure 2h,i). The interplanar spacing of C (002) plane observed in P-doped graphene slightly exceeds that of pristine LIG and is determined as ≈0.38 nm; this observation can be attributed to the elongated C─P bond length and larger atomic dimensions of the P atom in P-doped graphene.<sup>[38]</sup> The localized thermal hotspots generated during laser induction, and the rapid heating and cooling processes create uncertainty in the crystallization process, leading to the occurrence of crystalline and amorphous heterophase states of $\mathrm { C o / P } .$ -derived LIGs (Figure 2j). High-angle annular dark-field STEM images and the corresponding elemental mapping of $\mathrm { { C o } ( O _ { x } / P _ { x } ) }$ )@P-LIG indicate the uniform presence of Co in the metallic, phosphide, and oxide states (Figure 2l–o).

Raman analysis further demonstrates the ability of the ${ \mathrm { C o } } ( \mathrm { O } _ { \mathrm { x } } / \mathrm { P } _ { \mathrm { x } } )$ core in improving electron/ion transport by introducing additional electronic states. The reduced $I _ { \mathrm { D 1 } } / I _ { \mathrm { G } }$ ratio can be attributed to the formation of cobalt phosphide or oxide nanoparticles, subsequently reducing the vacancies in P-LIG. Thus, the resultant core–shell structure could potentially stabilize the doping state and mitigating the defects. The formation of ${ \mathrm { C o } } ( \mathrm { O } _ { \mathrm { x } } / \mathrm { P } _ { \mathrm { x } } )$ nanoparticles and the potential for defect remediation may diminish the original defect sites, and while themselves could act as new active sites. Modulated charge distribution and improved structural stability may result in a more stable performance during redox reactions.

The elemental forms of transition metals, as well as their oxides or phosphides, possess soft ferromagnetic properties, paving the way for an innovative transition from traditional electrochemical actuation to a highly eficient and controllable electromagnetic dual-mode actuation approach. The visual representation in Figure S22a (Supporting Information) illustrates the magnetic arrangement of $\mathrm { C o ( O _ { x } / P _ { x } ) @ P { \cdot } L I G }$ nanocomposites subjected to an external permanent magnet, presenting the possible arrangement of dipoles in the applied magnetic field. The large value of $\mathbf { M } _ { \mathrm { s } }$ can be attributed to the presence of ${ \mathrm { C o } } _ { 2 } { \mathrm { P } }$ and metallic Co having exceptional magnetic properties, as supported by XRD results. Similar properties are observed for $\mathrm { C o O _ { x } } @ \mathrm { L I G }$ nanomaterials owing to the presence of highly crystalline elemental Co. The equal atomic ratio for Co/P of $\mathrm { C o ( O _ { x } / P _ { x } ) @ P { \cdot } L I G }$ nanocomposite exhibits negligible magnetic properties, primarily due to the formation of CoP, a compound with semiconducting properties that contributes negligibly to magnetism. Additionally, the absence of magnetic transition metal phases prevents the formation of a strong magnetic phase, reducing the overall magnetic properties by disrupting the inherent magnetic structure.<sup>[74]</sup> The small M and H values observed in the optimized $\mathrm { C o } ( \mathrm { O _ { x } } / \mathrm { P _ { x } } ) @ \mathrm { P } .$ LIG indicates that such composites can magnetize and de magnetize under magnetic stimuli, facilitating precise and efortless control by applying magnetic fields (Figure S22b, Supporting Information).

The displacement of the actuator maintains a quasi-linear correlation with the magnitude of the magnetic field (inset to Figure 7b), indicating a high functional responsiveness. Initially, under 1 V DC, the actuation displacement reaches a value of ≈4 mm; then, the DC displacement nearly doubles under the influence of a 200 mT magnetic field, demonstrating significantly improved actuation performance. In modulating magnetic field, the developed actuator is shown to swiftly magnetize and demag netize (Figure 7d), demonstrating fast on–of switching capabil ities that can be utilized in complex actuation systems, such as soft robotics. The actuation durability of the actuator has also been investigated which stimulated using the unilateral magnetic stimulation for 1000 switching cycles, and the retention during the initial 600 cycles consistently remains above 90% (Figure 7e). However, mechanical fatigue develops in the actuator owing to sustained unilateral oscillations, results in reduced performance retention, and such issue can be mitigated by further adjusting the direction and frequency of the applied magnetic field. Notably, the actuation response waveform remains almost unchanged even after 1000 cycles. Based on the magnetic characteristics ofthe active electrode materials, an attempt was made to induce strain and deformation within scafolds by applying external magnetic fields. By pre-adjusting the position ofthe scafold to be downward, the unidirectional approach of the permanent magnet was utilized to induce biaxial strain in the platform. Upon gradually increasing the magnetic field to 200 mT, the scafold undergoes significant displacement deformation and changes its position from downward to upward, i.e., from an obtuse angle (171.5°) to a reflective angle (186.8°) (Figure 7f–h; Movie S5, Supporting Information), achieving a maximum strain change of 85 microstrains.

The demonstration of electric and magnetic actuation shed more light on the possibility offacilitating active and passive driving mechanisms for the MSCs culture platform, thereby holding promise for the further simplification ofits system complexity through the use of alternating electric or magnetic fields. By thinking the large-scale production to apply the dual-responsive actuated PCL platform to in vitro applications, there are still certain challenges for the future in elucidating mechanisms, large-scale deployment, and managing environmental conditions that need to be addressed to unlock the full potential of such soft actuators for biomedical applications. Although the actuation response and characteristics of the proposed actuator has been extensively evaluated using electrical, magnetic, chemical, and mechanical analysis methods, the complex transmission patterns of ion and electron intercalation and deintercalation at the electrode, detailed in Note S8 (Supporting Information), are yet to be completely understood. Thus, systematic studies on micro-actuation mechanisms are necessary to establish a theoretical foundation for understanding macroscale actuation behaviors. Further, the construction of functional modules by combining multiple PCL platforms to facilitate largescale production of scafolds is required. To further improve the productivity of PCL platforms, increasing the level of automation within the fabricated modules and integrating advanced flexible sensors for real-time monitoring and analysis is essential. Lastly, temperature and humidity fluctuations can afect the actuator response (Note S9, Supporting Information), necessitating further attention to heat and humidity management to promote cell viability and ensure smooth operation of the PCL platforms.

## 3. Conclusion

In summary, magnetic-responsive electro-ionic soft actuators based on novel Co/P co-doped nanoparticles with core–shell configurations incorporated into 3D graphene frameworks via single-step laser direct patterning have been developed. The physicochemical properties, electrochemical characteristics, and energy-band structures of the integrated $\mathrm { C o ( O _ { x } / P _ { x } ) @ P { \cdot } L I G }$ nanocomposites were thoroughly investigated to understand the underlying mechanisms for designing high-performance multiresponsive actuators. Structurally, the designed nanofiber-based heterointerface across electrodes and electrolytes significantly enhances high-eficient electron/ion transfer within the designed actuator. The active material exhibited a narrow magnetic hysteresis with saturated magnetization of $9 . 8 0 \mathrm { A m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { k g } ^ { - 1 }$ accompanied small remanence and coercivity values. The $\mathrm { C o } ( { \mathrm { O } _ { \mathrm { x } } } / { \mathrm { P } _ { \mathrm { x } } } ) @ \mathrm { P } .$ LIG-P.P actuators exhibited a high areal capacitance of 153.0 mF $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , resulting in significant improvement ofactuation performance with a peak-to-peak displacement of 13.08 mm under an ultra-low +0.5 V and doubled DC deflection under 200 mT, ultrafast response time of 1.38 s with exceptional stability (<10% degradation even after ≈106 000 cycles), detectable bending of≈280 μm even under ±10 mV. The promising demonstration of the soft actuator-supported automated platform, capable of providing mechanical strain and electrical stimuli simultaneously for the diferentiation and proliferation of MSCs, shows great potential of advancing biomedical soft robotics in the near future.
